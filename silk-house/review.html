Website review & proposal

Silk House Dining needs a website that matches the evening.

A review of silkhousedining.com, a working concept for what it could become, and a simple proposal for building it. Everything in the concept uses the restaurant's own words and photography, checked against the current site.

Summary

The restaurant is special. The website makes it hard to book.

Silk House has a lot to talk about: a chef's table for up to 24, a Japanese garden you built yourselves, a kitchen garden 100 yards from the kitchen, over 300 wines and an award in your first full year. The current site has most of the right information. The trouble is that it is spread over twelve pages and laid out like a long document, and on a phone there is no button, form or tappable number for making a booking.

First impression

Centred text on black with small thumbnails. The garden and the room barely feature.

Booking

No form, and no tap to call, email or WhatsApp links on any page.

Mobile

Header text is cut off, and the menu starts three screens down.

Content

Rich and personal, with plenty to work with.

Photography

A strong professional shoot, mixed with some third party stock images.

Search & technical

Page titles are in place, but Google is given no restaurant details and most images have no descriptions.

Worth keeping

What the current site gets right

  • Clear, honest prices on the menu page
  • A real menu, updated for the season
  • A warm, personal voice from Silke and Adrian
  • The award, the hygiene rating and a verbatim wedding review
  • Detailed wine, allergy and BYO policies
  • A professional photo shoot of the room, food and garden
  • Page titles and meta descriptions on every page
  • Strong local story: Tonbridge Brewery, Bean Smitten, Herbert Hall

Findings

Eleven things holding it back

High

Booking is the hardest thing on the site to do

For a reservation only restaurant, booking is the whole job of the website. There is no booking form, and I found no tap to call, email or WhatsApp links on any page: the phone number and email are plain text, so on a phone a guest has to copy or retype them. The rules a guest needs (book at least a day ahead, a deposit secures the table, Sunday lunch has a minimum of six to eight, you choose your menu in advance) are scattered across the home, Book, FAQ and Terms pages.

In the concept: a "Request a table" panel with date, sitting, guests, occasion and dietary notes. It checks the opening rules as you go (it warns about Sunday evenings and small Sunday lunches, blocks same day dates) and opens WhatsApp or email with a neat, complete message ready for Silke. Silke still handles every booking personally, as now (the FAQ says bookings are not taken through any online system, and the concept keeps it that way). Guests just no longer have to piece the rules together. Every phone number, email and WhatsApp link is tappable.

High

The mobile experience is broken in places

On a phone the email and phone number in the header are clipped behind the menu bar, and the logo sits in a white box on a grey strip. On the menu page the menu itself begins about 2,600px down, after three screens of photographs. On the FAQ page, the heading and the terms note are moved to the very bottom, about 4,500px down, and the questions appear in a different order from desktop.

In the concept: designed for the phone first, with a sticky "Request a table" button, a full screen menu, and the menu text straight after its heading.

Medium

The FAQ page looks unfinished

Each question sits in its own block in the browser's default typeface, with a plain grey "Learn More" button and large blank gaps between them. The answers do open, but the page looks as if its styling failed to load, on exactly the page a nervous first time guest will read.

In the concept: fourteen questions, answered from the restaurant's own FAQ, Terms and allergy policy, in a simple open and close list that matches the rest of the site.

Medium

Policies that disagree with each other

The FAQ says a deposit can be refunded with at least a week's notice if the table can be rebooked. The Terms say cancellations within 28 calendar days are not refundable unless the places can be resold. The Book page says lunch opens at 12.30, while the Terms say the gates open at 12.15. A guest who reads one and is held to the other will feel misled.

In the concept: neither notice period is quoted, and the FAQ points to the Terms instead. Worth settling before launch so the site can give one clear answer.

Medium

Out of date content

The Events page still leads with the tasting on Wednesday 23 September 2026 as upcoming, and on a phone that event sits above the page's own heading, which is 1,786px down. By 9 October a guest could easily think the site is not being kept up to date, which does the restaurant a disservice.

In the concept: events are presented as a series ("at least six a year", with the regions coming up), so the page never goes stale between dates.

Medium

The best assets are shown at thumbnail size

The garden is the thing nobody else in Kent has, yet on the home page it is barely visible. Photographs sit in small boxes either side of centred paragraphs. Five or more typefaces are in use (Playfair Display, Roboto Slab, Inter, Libre Franklin, DM Sans, with Times New Roman as a fallback), and the mix makes the pages feel inconsistent.

In the concept: two typefaces, a palette taken from the heron logo (ink, gold, rice paper), the dining room view full screen on arrival, and a "four seasons" garden section with the season's kanji and gently falling blossom, fireflies, leaves or snow.

Medium

Some images appear to be third party

A few images carry file names from other sources: recipe photographs from Serious Eats (grilled sardines, a steak, clams with XO sauce) and a Pixabay stock image of broccoli. On a site that states "copyright all text, design & images", using other people's photographs is a risk, and stock food photos undersell a kitchen with its own professional shoot.

In the concept: none of these are used. Every photograph is the restaurant's own.

Medium

Navigation has twelve items

Home, Book, Menu, Wine, Chef's Table, Private Dining, Weddings, Events, Kitchen Garden, Japanese Garden, FAQs, Location. That is a lot to scan on a phone, and "Book" is styled the same as everything else.

In the concept: six items (Experience, Menu, Garden, Wine, Occasions, Visit) plus a gold Reserve button that is always visible.

Medium

Google is told very little

The structured data describes a generic "WebSite", not a restaurant, so search engines get no opening hours, cuisine, price range, address or reservation details from it. The page addresses are long and keyword stuffed, for example /restaurant-in-mid-kent-chefs-table-in-kent-near-paddock-wood-silk-house-dining.

In the concept: full Restaurant schema (hours, cuisine, address, award, reservations). In the real build: short page addresses, with redirects from every old address so no existing Google ranking is lost.

Low

Accessibility

14 of the 16 images on the home page, and 28 of 30 on the menu page, have no alternative text, so screen readers and Google Images get nothing. Body text is centred throughout, which is harder to read in long paragraphs.

In the concept: descriptive alt text on every image, left aligned reading text, keyboard focus states, 44px touch targets, and all motion switched off for visitors who ask their device for reduced motion.

Low

Small copy slips

"Sommeler" (sommelier), "sparking" (sparkling, twice), "Crèmante" (Crémant), "Canon Street" (Cannon Street), "compté" (Comté) and "paddock Wood". Small, but on a fine dining site they get noticed.

In the concept: corrected, and long paragraphs tightened without losing Silke's voice.

The concept

What has been built

A single, fast page that tells the story in the order a guest wants it: what is this, what will I eat, where is it, and how do I book. Much of it moves, so here it is in motion.

The plan

Three phases, one step at a time

Nothing about how you take bookings has to change to launch. Online booking can wait until there are a few months of real numbers to decide it with.

Phase 1

Launch

Weeks 1 to 4

  • Go through the concept together and agree any changes
  • Build it as proper pages: Menu, Japanese Garden, Kitchen Garden, Wine and Events, Weddings, Private Dining, Visit and FAQs
  • The table request form, arriving by WhatsApp or email as now
  • Every phone number, email and WhatsApp link tappable
  • Redirects from every old address, restaurant details for Google, Google Business Profile tidy up
  • Tested on phones, tablets and computers, then launched
Phase 2

Grow

Months 2 to 3

  • A simple editor so you can change the weekly menu and events yourselves in minutes
  • Counting table requests, menu views and where visitors come from
  • Fill the photo gaps: an autumn garden shoot and Adrian at the pass
Phase 3

Online booking

Later, only if wanted

  • Decided using a few months of real request numbers from Phases 1 and 2
  • A reservations system taking deposits online, styled to match the site
  • Quoted separately at the time
