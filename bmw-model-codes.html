BMW badges, decoded

Nearly every chart explaining BMW's names was written for America, where diesel barely exists and a Touring is called a wagon. Over here a 320i, an X3 20d and an iX3 40 sit on the same forecourt, half the used stock is diesel or a plug in hybrid, and the same letter means two opposite things depending on where it sits. This is the UK version, and I check it against what is actually on the forecourt.

Dan Cane
BMW Sales Executive · Hedin Ruxley, South East

A BMW badge is not a name, it is a short sentence. Once you can read it you can walk down a row of cars and know, without opening a single door, which one is bigger, which one is quicker, which one takes diesel and which one drives all four wheels. It takes about four minutes to learn and it saves you being sold something on the strength of a letter.

1. The first number is the family

On a car, the leading digit is the series and it tracks size and price, not speed. A 1 Series is the small hatchback, a 7 Series is the limousine, and everything else lives in between. Small ones can be the quick ones: an M2 will embarrass most 5 Series.

Where the simple rule bends

Even does not reliably mean two doors. A 2 Series Gran Coupé and a 4 Series Gran Coupé both have four, and the 2 Series Active Tourer is a tall five seat family car. Read even as "the style led version of the odd one next door" and you will be right far more often than you are wrong.

2. The X cars, and what a Coupé means when it has four doors

An X is BMW's SUV line and the number tracks size and price in exactly the same way, from the X1 up to the X7. The odd and even split carries over, with a twist that catches people out.

Odd is an SAV

Sports Activity Vehicle

X1
Compact, the entry price
X3

X5

X7
Full size, flagship price

Even is an SAC

Sports Activity Coupé

X2
The X1 with a sloping roof
X4
The X3 with a sloping roof
X6
The X5 with a sloping roof

BMW calls the even ones coupés for the roofline, not the door count. An X4 has four doors and a boot like an X3 does. What you give up is a little headroom in the back and some boot height. What you get is the shape. That is genuinely the whole trade, and it is worth sitting in the back of both before you decide.

Two models sit outside the grid. The XM is BMW M's own plug in hybrid SUV rather than a numbered X car, and the iX is its own electric model, not the electric version of an X something. That last one matters, because iX1 and iX3 are electric versions of the X1 and X3.

See them on the forecourt: X1, X3, X5, every SAC in stock.

3. The middle number is power, not engine size

This is the one almost everybody has wrong, and it is easy to prove. Take BMW's own figures for the brand new cars sitting at Ruxley as I write this: the 220i, the 320i, the 420i and the 520i all use the same 1,998cc engine. Four different badges, one engine. Meanwhile a 120 is 1,499cc, so a car wearing a 20 can be a 1.5. The number is not litres and has not been for years.

What it actually tells you is where that car sits inside its own family, roughly like this:

TierWhat it means in that range
16, 18, 20The way in. Most of what you see on a forecourt.
23, 25, 30The middle. Noticeably stronger, still sensible to run.
35, 40Properly quick without being an M car.
45, 50, 60The top of that range.
Only compare within one family

The tier climbs with the car, so the same number means more power the bigger the BMW it is written on. Across the group's used stock a 120 makes about 170 horsepower, a 320i about 182 and a 520i about 207, and all three are 20 cars. A 540i is not a 340i with a bigger badge, it is a different engine. The honest rule is that the number ranks a car against its own brothers and tells you nothing at all across families.

Diesel is the neat exception that proves it: a 120d, a 320d and a 520d all make the same 189 horsepower, because it is the same engine doing three different jobs.

This is the trap. The same letter means petrol at the end and electric at the front, and nothing but its position tells you which. A 320i takes unleaded. An i4 has no engine in it at all. If you remember one thing off this page, make it that one, because it is the mistake I correct most often and it is an expensive one to make on a used car advert.

The American charts will also tell you diesel is not sold. That is true in America and not remotely true here: diesel is a third of the used BMW stock around me and BMW still builds plenty of it, up to and including an M Performance diesel in the M340d. If a guide tells you a d badge is exotic, it was not written for this country.

5. sDrive, xDrive and eDrive

This part of the badge is about which wheels are driven, and it is the bit worth understanding if you have a steep driveway or tow anything.

xDrive

All four wheels are driven. Better traction in the wet and on a cold morning, a little more weight and usually a little more fuel.

sDrive

Two wheels are driven, and which two depends on the car. An X1 sDrive drives its front wheels. A 2 Series Coupé drives its rear ones. The badge says "not all four" and stops there, so check the listing rather than assume.

eDrive

The electric cars use their own word for two wheel drive, as in i4 eDrive40 and iX2 eDrive20. An electric BMW with all four wheels driven still says xDrive, as in iX xDrive40.

Every card on my stock page prints the driven wheels, so you never have to work it out from the badge.

6. The M ladder, and why an M Sport is not an M car

Three quite different things wear the letter M, and the gap between the bottom and the top of this list is enormous. This is the single most common misunderstanding I clear up across the desk, usually when somebody has paid an M car price expectation for an M Sport trim.

M Sport

320i M Sport · X3 20d M Sport

A trim level. The bodykit, the wheels, usually a firmer suspension set up and better seats and steering wheel. The engine is the ordinary one for that car. It is by far the most common specification in the UK, which is exactly why an M badge on the boot lid proves nothing on its own. M Sport Pro adds more of the same look, again without touching the engine.

M Performance

M135 · M235 · M340i · M440i · X3 M50

The letter and a number together, either in front of the tier on a car or after the model on an X. A genuinely more powerful engine with its own brakes, suspension and exhaust work. Quick and quietly so. An M340i makes around 374 horsepower against the 182 of the 320i it is based on.

A full M car

M2 · M3 · M4 · M5

M, then a number, and nothing else. Developed by BMW M, engine and chassis both, and priced accordingly. An M3 is around 510 horsepower and the current M5 is around 726. Nothing with an extra letter after the number is one of these.

Two things the old charts get wrong here

An M car is not automatically petrol any more. The current M5 is a plug in hybrid, and there is an M Performance diesel in the M340d. And M Performance cars do get chassis work, so "engine only" is too tidy a line to draw. The reliable test is the name: M with a number and nothing else is an M car, M with a number plus anything else is not.

Have a look at the other two: M135 and M135i, full M cars.

8. The letters BMW has started dropping

Current BMWs are quietly losing the fuel letter on petrol models. A new 1 Series is badged 120 where the last one was a 120i. The M Performance version is now M135 rather than M135i. The new X3 is an X3 20 xDrive rather than an X3 20i.

Both are petrol. On a used forecourt you will see the old badge and the new one parked next to each other, which looks like a difference and is not one: read the missing i as a change of house style and nothing more. Diesels have kept their d.

Where this stops being reliable

BMW moves the goalposts every few years, so treat all of the above as a very good guide rather than a law. There are oddities that fit none of it: the 128ti is a front wheel drive hot hatch with its own suffix, Alpina cars run a different naming system altogether, and the electric range is still settling down. Every figure on this page I have checked against BMW's own data for the cars in front of me, and I have deliberately not told you which models have been discontinued, because the charts that prompted this page made two such claims and I have cars on the forecourt that contradict both.

What does the 20 in a BMW 320i mean?

It is a power tier, not the engine size. The 3 says 3 Series, the 20 says where it sits in that range and the i says petrol. BMW's own figures for the new cars at Ruxley show the 220i, 320i, 420i and 520i all using one 1,998cc engine, and a 120 using a 1,499cc one, so the number cannot be read as litres.

Why is the i on a 320i petrol but the i on an iX3 electric?

Position decides it. At the end of a badge, i means petrol. At the front, it means the car is fully electric, as in i4, i5, i7, iX, iX1, iX2 and iX3. Same letter, opposite meaning. Note that iX is its own model rather than the electric version of an X car, while iX1 and iX3 are.

Is an M Sport BMW a real M car?

No. M Sport is a trim: styling, wheels, usually firmer suspension and better seats, on the ordinary engine. M Performance cars carry M and a number together, such as M135 or X3 M50, and are genuinely more powerful. A full M car is M followed by a number and nothing else, such as M2, M3, M4 or M5.

What is the difference between sDrive and xDrive?

xDrive drives all four wheels. sDrive drives two, and which two depends on the car: an X1 sDrive drives the front, a 2 Series Coupé drives the rear. Fully electric BMWs badge their two wheel drive versions eDrive instead, and keep xDrive for all wheel drive.

What is the difference between an X3 and an X4?

They are the same size of car. The X4 has a sloping roofline, which BMW calls a Sports Activity Coupé, so you lose a little rear headroom and boot height and gain the shape. It still has four doors.

Why do some new BMWs have no i, like the 120 or the M135?

BMW has started dropping the fuel letter from petrol models. A current 1 Series is a 120 where the last was a 120i, and the M Performance version is M135 rather than M135i. Both are petrol. Diesels have kept their d.
