Why BMW EV

Why people choose a BMW electric car.

BMW electric vehicles include three-year unlimited mileage vehicle cover, plus high-voltage battery protection for up to 8 years or 100,000 miles from first registration, whichever comes first.

That gives buyers confidence on both everyday ownership and long-term battery protection. If you are still weighing up petrol, hybrid, plug-in hybrid and full electric, this is where the real-life numbers start to matter.

Charging reality

Charging is much easier than most people think.

118,000+

Public chargers across the UK at more than 45,000 locations, and the number is still growing rapidly.

8,329

Petrol stations left in the UK. The charging network has already overtaken fuel infrastructure in reach and is pulling further ahead each year.

Most EV owners do the majority of their charging at home overnight, which means they start every day with a full charge and rarely think about it at all. Public chargers are there when you need them, not as a daily necessity.

Through your business

If you run a company, electric is where the maths gets interesting.

This is the part most people don't realise until they sit down and work it through. Putting an electric BMW through a limited company, or taking one via salary sacrifice, can cost a fraction of what the same car costs a petrol driver once tax is accounted for. Here's why, in plain terms.

4%

Benefit-in-Kind for 2026/27. A petrol or diesel company car typically sits between 25% and 37%. On a £45,000 BMW that gap alone can mean thousands a year less tax on your payslip. The rate is fixed and rises just one point a year to 9% by 2029/30, so you can plan ahead.

100%

First Year Allowance on a new EV. Buy a brand-new electric BMW outright through the business and the full cost can be written off against your profits in year one, currently available until 31 March 2027. A used EV doesn't qualify the same way, so timing and spec matter.

Salary sacrifice. If your employer offers it, you give up part of your gross salary for the car, so you're paying out of pre-tax income. Drivers commonly save somewhere around a fifth to a half of the equivalent cost, and the only tax due is that low Benefit-in-Kind figure.

No fuel benefit charge, plus the charging perks. There's no taxable fuel benefit on a company EV the way there is on petrol, charging at the workplace is currently treated as tax-free, and there's relief available on installing a charge point too.

Tax treatment depends on your own circumstances and the rules can change, so treat this as a starting point rather than advice. Dan will walk you through your situation and, where it helps, point you toward proper accountancy guidance before you commit.

Common questions

Things people usually ask Dan first.

How long is the BMW battery warranty?

BMW electric vehicles include three-year unlimited mileage vehicle cover, plus high-voltage battery protection for up to 8 years or 100,000 miles from first registration, whichever comes first.

Do I need home charging to make it work?

It helps, but plenty of people manage without it. If you have a driveway or garage, a home charger makes the whole thing easier. If not, Dan will help you work out a charging routine that fits your life.

Is public charging actually reliable now?

It is far better than it was. The UK now has over 118,000 public chargers, and rapid chargers on motorways are improving quickly. Most EV owners rarely rely on public charging for day-to-day driving.

What about longer journeys. Will I make it?

That depends on your mileage and the model you choose. Dan will go through your actual routes with you, not a theoretical scenario. Most people are pleasantly surprised once they see the numbers.

I am not ready to commit yet. Is that okay?

Completely fine. This is here to help you work it out at your own pace. Dan is not going to push you towards something that does not make sense for your situation.

Can I use my current car as a part exchange?

Yes, and that is often the cleanest way to move into an EV. Dan can give you a realistic idea of what your car is worth and whether the switch makes financial sense before you do anything.
